# Filament Inventory MVP

Status: ready-for-agent

## Problem Statement

A hobbyist needs a dependable way to know where every filament spool is and how much material remains. Physical movement between storage slots and material slots is easy to forget, so a digital inventory can drift away from reality unless recording a movement is faster and clearer than relying on memory. The hobbyist also needs to recover deliberately when the recorded assignment is wrong instead of hiding inconsistencies or creating ambiguous swaps.

The first release must work as a private, local-first tool without an account, cloud service, or mandatory NFC hardware. At the same time, NFC is part of the MVP from the first implementation increment: storage-slot tags must make the common workflow faster, launch or foreground the Android app where the platform permits it, and remain safe because a scan only opens context and never changes inventory by itself.

## Solution

Build a Flutter mobile app whose first public target is Android and Google Play. It manages one local inventory of filament spools, storage slots, material units, and material slots. The app makes the Scan home the primary entry point, while persistent Home, Spools, Places, and Archive navigation keeps every workflow available without NFC.

An active filament spool is always Stored, Loaded, or Unlocated. Confirmed actions move it atomically between compatible unoccupied slots, update its remaining quantity when required, and append durable history. Explicit recovery actions handle occupied destinations, missing spools, wrong assignments, consumption, retirement, and mistaken archival without silently discarding information.

NFC support ships in the first Android MVP. A storage-slot tag contains one application-owned HTTPS storage-slot reference. Foreground scanning and Android App Link delivery open a read-only storage-slot context; the hobbyist must still choose an action, review the complete change, and confirm it. The same operation is always reachable manually. All inventory data and identifier lookup remain on the device.

## Implementation Decisions

- **Product scope and targets:** Build one shared Flutter codebase. Android and Google Play are the first release targets. The architecture must preserve a portable Flutter implementation for a future iOS release, but an iOS App Store release is not required for the MVP.
- **Initial NFC commitment:** NFC reading, storage-slot tag registration, and Android App Link routing are part of the first implementation increment and the first Android MVP. They are not optional follow-up features. The app may ship the NFC path only after the early Pixel hardware gate passes; manual workflows remain available regardless.
- **Navigation model:** Use Scan home as the primary experience. Keep Home, Spools, Places, and Archive persistently reachable. Do not carry the prototype's variant switcher, scenario lab, or state inspector into production.
- **Context-first interaction:** A storage-slot scan or manual selection opens the same read-only storage-slot context. Occupied contexts emphasize the occupant and check out. Empty contexts emphasize check in, place, and register. Mutating operations use focused guided sheets that collect inputs, present a complete review, and commit only on the final explicit confirmation.
- **Application boundaries:** Separate the inventory rules and transactional use cases, local persistence, Flutter presentation, NFC reader/writer integration, storage-slot-reference parsing, and incoming-link routing. Platform packages remain behind application-owned interfaces so package replacement and deterministic test inputs do not leak into inventory rules.
- **Single local inventory:** One installation owns exactly one local inventory. No login, tenant, remote source of truth, or server reconciliation exists.
- **Stable identities:** Filament spools, storage slots, material units, and material slots have opaque stable internal identities that are never reused or changed. Storage areas are free-form labels on storage slots, not entities. Individual physical NFC tags are not entities.
- **Current-state authority:** Current entity records are the source of truth. History supplements those records and is not replayed to reconstruct state; the application is not event sourced.
- **Filament-spool data:** Require material type, normalized `#RRGGBB` filament color, and a positive whole-gram remaining quantity for every active filament spool. Permit an optional spool label, manufacturer, product name, original nominal quantity, and notes. Do not track diameter or generate a user-visible inventory number. Spool labels are optional and need not be unique.
- **Location data:** A storage slot has a name, optional storage-area label, archive state, and optional occupant. A material unit has a name, archive state, and one or more material slots. A material slot belongs to exactly one material unit and has a name, archive state, and optional occupant. Connection of a material unit to a printer is not represented.
- **Visible-name uniqueness:** Active material-unit names are unique. Active material-slot names are unique within their material unit. Active storage-slot names are unique within their optional storage-area label. Comparisons trim surrounding whitespace and ignore letter case while preserving chosen display spelling. Archived records do not reserve names. Restoration pauses on a conflict rather than changing an identity or name automatically.
- **State invariant:** An active filament spool is exactly one of Stored, Loaded, or Unlocated. A Stored filament spool is assigned to exactly one storage slot; a Loaded filament spool is assigned to exactly one material slot; an Unlocated filament spool is assigned to neither. Consumed and Retired filament spools are archived and unassigned. Each storage slot and material slot has at most one occupant.
- **Normal transition contract:** Place permits Unlocated to Stored or Loaded. Check out permits Stored to Loaded. Check in permits Loaded to Stored. Storage movement permits Stored to Stored. Material-slot movement permits Loaded to Loaded. A normal transition requires an unoccupied compatible destination, vacates the source, assigns the destination, and appends history atomically.
- **Quantity contract:** Leaving a material slot requires confirmation or update of the remaining quantity. Pure storage movement does not change quantity. An active quantity must remain a positive whole number of grams. Entering zero starts a separate consumption confirmation rather than persisting an active zero-gram filament spool.
- **Correct assignment contract:** Normal movement to an occupied destination is blocked. A separate confirmed Correct assignment action shows the selected filament spool and current destination occupant, vacates the selected spool's recorded source, makes a different recorded occupant Unlocated, assigns the selected spool, and records all effects atomically. It never performs an implicit swap.
- **Other recovery actions:** Permit an explicit correction from Stored or Loaded to Unlocated, quantity correction without movement, registration of an unknown physical filament spool, retirement of a missing filament spool, and restoration of mistaken archival. Unlocated filament spools remain visible until deliberately resolved. No bulk-audit mode is required.
- **Consumption, retirement, and restoration:** Consume starts from any active state, records zero grams, vacates an assignment, and enters Consumed. Retire starts from any active state, requires a reason covering damaged, lost, or deliberately removed spools, preserves the last known positive quantity, vacates an assignment, and enters Retired. Restore always enters Unlocated and never reclaims a prior slot. Restoring Consumed requires a new positive quantity; restoring Retired confirms or corrects its positive quantity.
- **Place lifecycle:** Storage slots and material slots can be archived only while unoccupied. A material unit can be archived only when all of its material slots are unoccupied; archiving it hides the unit and all its slots without changing each slot's individual archive state. Restoring the unit reveals only material slots that were not individually archived. Domain records are never permanently deleted.
- **History contract:** Registration, movement, remaining-quantity changes, corrections, consumption, retirement, and restoration append immutable chronological entries. Each entry records its time, action, affected stable identities, and relevant before and after values. Corrections append rather than rewrite. Descriptive-field edits need preserve only current values. History is retained for archived filament spools and shown in filament-spool details; there is no global activity feed.
- **Transactional persistence:** All confirmed changes update current records and history in one local atomic transaction. Drafts, navigation, scans, link deliveries, cancellation, validation failures, process interruption before commit, and failed NFC writes do not change inventory.
- **Persistence technology:** The implementer may choose the local database or object store, provided it enforces the model, transactions, uniqueness, schema versioning, migrations, and test seam in this specification. The representation carries a schema version. Migrations are atomic. A failed migration or unreadable store preserves prior data, blocks inventory mutation, and displays retry or diagnostic recovery; the app never silently clears or recreates inventory.
- **NFC package boundary:** Start with the qualified baseline of `nfc_manager` 4.2.1 and `nfc_manager_ndef` 1.1.0 for foreground NDEF reading and writing. Pin compatible versions behind an application-owned NFC interface. If real-hardware qualification exposes a blocker, replace the package behind that interface without changing inventory use cases or UI semantics.
- **Supported tag contract:** Standardize on compatible, preformatted, writable NFC Forum Type 2 tags with enough capacity for the canonical URI. Do not depend on tag hardware UIDs because they may be absent or unstable. Do not require the app to format blank tags or make tags permanently read-only. Replace tags that are unformatted, read-only, too small, or unreliable in representative placement.
- **Canonical NDEF payload:** Register a tag with a single NFC Forum well-known URI record whose value is `https://<stable-owned-domain-or-subdomain>/s#v1.<storage-slot-id>`. The URI record is first and is the complete canonical payload. The identifier is the storage slot's opaque stable identity encoded in an agreed URL-safe form. The version prefix permits later parser evolution.
- **NFC registration:** Registration starts from a selected storage slot, checks NFC availability, opens a foreground session, reports tag compatibility and write status, warns before replacing the complete NDEF message, and requires confirmation before writing. A successful write changes no spool state, occupancy, or history. Any number of tags may contain the same storage-slot reference.
- **NFC read results:** The application-owned NFC interface distinguishes unavailable or disabled NFC, user cancellation, incompatible or unformatted tag, read-only tag when writing, interrupted I/O, foreign or blank content, malformed FilaManager reference, valid known storage slot, valid Unknown storage slot, and unexpected failure. Feedback explicitly states when inventory remains unchanged and offers retry or manual navigation where useful.
- **Storage-slot-reference validation:** Validate the exact HTTPS scheme, production host, `/s` path, `v1` fragment prefix, and identifier syntax before local lookup. Do not accept a custom scheme, arbitrary host, hardware UID, MIME payload, Android Application Record, or server lookup as the canonical identity path.
- **Read-only delivery:** Foreground scans, Android App Links, copied links, repeated scans, and duplicate lifecycle deliveries only navigate. They never reserve a destination or invoke an inventory mutation. Warm and cold launch paths feed the same validated route and local lookup.
- **Android App Link:** Configure a verified, browsable HTTPS App Link for the exact production host and `/s` route. The app must handle both initial and subsequent links. Host an association file containing the final Android application ID and every required signing-certificate fingerprint, including the Play App Signing certificate.
- **Static web host:** The stable public HTTPS host contains platform association files and a safe `/s` fallback page. It has no account system, API, inventory data, analytics requirement, or server-side identifier mapping. Because the storage-slot identifier is in the URI fragment, it is not sent in the HTTP request. The identifier is nevertheless an identifier, not a secret.
- **Platform launch behavior:** On Android 16, an unlocked-device scan may cold-start or foreground the app through the verified App Link. Android 17 presents an open-link notification that requires a tap. Locked devices, a force-stopped app, a never-launched app, or user-disabled NFC/link handling may require unlock or manual launch. The UI must not promise behavior the operating system controls. If the app is absent, the browser opens the static fallback.
- **Unknown content distinction:** Foreign, blank, and malformed tags are Unknown tag and can be reported only during an explicit foreground scan because they cannot launch FilaManager through the canonical App Link. A syntactically valid FilaManager reference whose stable identity is absent from local data is Unknown storage slot. It remains unusable until the tag is registered again or matching local data is restored in a future backup feature.
- **Android NFC configuration:** Declare the normal NFC permission and declare NFC hardware as optional. Check adapter presence and enabled state at runtime. Do not filter non-NFC devices out of Play and do not disable manual workflows when NFC is unavailable.
- **iOS preparation:** Keep the NFC and URI contracts portable and publish the future iOS association file alongside the Android association file when practical, so tags do not need rewriting later. Native iOS entitlement, usage description, Universal Link handling, and iPhone hardware qualification are required before a future iOS release, not before the first Android release.
- **Accessibility:** Controls use semantic labels and usable touch targets. State, occupancy, validation, success, and failure never rely on color alone. Guided-sheet opening and closing uses deliberate focus movement, and important success or failure feedback is announced to screen readers.
- **Android platform behavior:** Target Android 16/API 36 or the higher level required at submission. Support enforced edge-to-edge layout, predictive back, and adaptive behavior at widths of at least 600 dp. Avoid orientation, aspect-ratio, or resizability assumptions that current Android ignores.
- **Release artifact:** Produce a signed Android App Bundle with a monotonically increasing version code and enroll it in Play App Signing. Retain the upload key securely. The final artifact must satisfy 64-bit and 16 KB memory-page compatibility requirements, including all native transitive dependencies.
- **Local-only privacy:** Include no account, advertising, analytics, crash reporting, telemetry, remote logging, or other SDK behavior that transmits user or device data in the first release. Disable Android cloud backup or explicitly exclude the inventory store and equivalent sensitive local data so the product can truthfully claim local-only storage. Device-to-device behavior and any remaining backed-up configuration must match the privacy policy.
- **Play disclosures:** Audit the final dependency graph, merged manifest, permissions, and observed network behavior before declaring that no data is collected or shared. Publish a compliant public privacy policy and make it accessible in the app. Complete content rating, target audience, ads, app access, health, financial, support contact, store listing, country, and price declarations according to the final bundle and product choices.
- **Play account gates:** Establish the developer account and final package name early. Complete identity, contact, physical-device, and package-name verification required by the account. If the personal account was created after 13 November 2023, complete the required closed test with at least 12 continuously opted-in testers for at least 14 days and obtain production access.
- **Release sequencing:** Deliver an internal Play build first, qualify its NFC and App Link behavior on the Pixel 9, inspect Play's bundle and pre-review results, complete any required closed test, and use managed publishing for production. Recheck current target API, policies, account tasks, package registration, review lead times, and Play Console warnings immediately before submission.

## Testing Decisions

- **Test philosophy:** Tests assert behavior visible at the application or platform boundary, not widget structure, state-management calls, database table layout, or NFC-package implementation details. A good test describes the starting inventory and external event, performs the same choices and confirmations available to the hobbyist, and verifies the resulting screen, persisted inventory, occupancy, quantity, and history. It also verifies unchanged state whenever a flow is cancelled, interrupted, invalid, or unconfirmed.
- **Primary automated seam:** Use one high app-level Flutter integration seam. Launch the real production app with a temporary production persistence store and an application-owned fake NFC/incoming-link adapter, then drive behavior through the rendered UI. This is the default seam for registration, navigation, search, all movement and recovery actions, archival, history, persistence across restart, NFC result presentation, cold/warm link routing, idempotent delivery, and accessibility semantics.
- **NFC is not faked out of the product:** The fake adapter exists only to make the high-level suite deterministic. The production NFC reader/writer and Android App Link path are implemented in the first increment and must pass the physical qualification gate before the first Android release.
- **Core workflow coverage:** At the primary seam, cover registration into every legal initial state; occupied and empty storage-slot contexts; check out; check in with changed quantity; Stored-to-Stored and Loaded-to-Loaded movement; placement from Unlocated; quantity correction; explicit transition to Unlocated; consumption; retirement; restoration; place and material-unit archival; and browsing active and archived records.
- **Invariant and conflict coverage:** Verify that every active state has the correct assignment cardinality, each slot has at most one occupant, archived filament spools occupy no slot, normal moves reject occupied destinations, Correct assignment displaces the recorded occupant to Unlocated without swapping, invalid names and quantities are rejected, and each accepted multi-record action is atomic.
- **Confirmation and failure coverage:** Verify that scans, links, drafts, review screens, back navigation, cancellation, validation errors, repeated deliveries, forced UI disposal before confirmation, NFC cancellation, NFC unavailability, unknown content, and simulated read/write errors leave inventory unchanged. Verify that messages say this and expose the agreed retry or manual path.
- **Persistence and migration coverage:** Run confirmed actions against the real temporary store, restart the app, and verify current state plus immutable history. Exercise supported schema migrations with representative prior stores. Inject migration and unreadable-store failures and verify that prior bytes remain intact, mutations are blocked, no empty inventory is created, and retry or diagnostics are visible.
- **NFC parser and writer coverage:** Through the application boundary, verify strict acceptance of the production HTTPS host, `/s`, supported version, and valid identifier; rejection of wrong schemes, hosts, paths, versions, missing fragments, malformed identifiers, foreign payloads, and blank messages; exact generation of the canonical single-record NDEF message; and distinction between Unknown tag and Unknown storage slot.
- **Accessibility coverage:** Include semantics assertions for primary controls and messages, keyboard or assistive focus behavior around guided sheets, non-color state cues, and announcements for success and unchanged-state failures. Supplement automated checks with a screen-reader walkthrough on the release device.
- **Early physical NFC gate:** Before building later inventory features on an assumed hardware path, run the production NFC adapter on the Pixel 9 with representative storage-slot tags and placement. Confirm the exact tag type and NDEF state, write the canonical storage-slot reference, read it repeatedly, and complete ten fresh reads in representative placement. The target is every read succeeding, normally within two seconds and never over five seconds.
- **Physical NFC failure matrix:** On the Pixel 9, exercise user cancellation, NFC disabled, read-only NDEF, unformatted tag, insufficient or incompatible tag where available, interrupted write using a sacrificial tag, immediate retry, repeated scan, app restart, and representative alternative placement. No case may show stale success, write an unintended reference, or change inventory. If the intended tags or placement fail, replace them or the placement; if the package fails, replace it behind the application-owned interface.
- **Cross-platform tag evidence:** The first Android release is not blocked by an unavailable iPhone, but tags and payload remain provisional for iOS until an iPhone 17e reads Android-written tags, writes a tag the Pixel reads, completes the repeated-placement matrix, and passes cancellation, read-only, unformatted, and interrupted-session cases. That qualification is mandatory before relying on NFC in a future iOS release.
- **App Link qualification:** Test association using the final package name and Play App Signing fingerprint. Verify warm, backgrounded, normally terminated, force-stopped, never-launched, locked, unlocked, NFC-disabled, offline, and user-disabled link-handling states as the platform allows. Verify malformed and unknown local identifiers, repeated delivery, browser fallback when the app is absent, and read-only navigation in every successful delivery. Exercise real NFC routing on physical Android hardware; command-line and emulator link tests do not replace a real tag scan.
- **Play-delivered release gate:** Upload the signed API-36 AAB to internal testing and inspect the merged manifest, permissions, signing, supported devices, 64-bit binaries, 16 KB compatibility, and automated pre-review results. Test the Play-installed build on the Pixel 9 for the complete manual and NFC workflows, edge-to-edge, predictive back, adaptive layout, disabled backup behavior, offline operation, and absence of unexpected network traffic.
- **Acceptance validation:** Use the app personally for four weeks. The MVP passes product validation when the digital inventory remains aligned with physical reality and the hobbyist can find any filament spool's location, state, and remaining quantity within ten seconds.
- **Prior art:** The repository has no production test suite yet. Reuse the behavioral scenarios and safety expectations from the throwaway Scan home prototype and the physical matrix from the [NFC tag spike](https://github.com/Tynamix/filamanager/tree/e23c6e1fa5e0da802597ee80891071471da684c6/.scratch/filament-inventory/prototypes/nfc-tag-spike), but do not reuse their implementation code. The spike's current Pixel inspection of a writable 492-byte Type 2, NTAG215-compatible tag is provisional evidence only; its write, placement, recovery, and iPhone matrices remain incomplete.

## Out of Scope

- Mandatory accounts, cloud synchronization, remote inventory storage, shared or multi-user inventories, and server-side storage-slot lookup
- User-visible export, import, backup restoration, device transfer, merge behavior, and imports into a non-empty inventory
- An iOS App Store release in the first MVP; future iOS NFC and Universal Link preparation remains part of the portable architecture
- Continuous or silent background NFC scanning; operating-system dispatch of a scanned HTTPS tag is supported but is not background scanning by the app
- Tracking, authenticating, password-protecting, or inventorying individual physical tags; using hardware UIDs; formatting blank tags; or making tags permanently read-only
- Printers, relationships between material units and printers, printer control, slicer or G-code integration, and automatic consumption calculation
- Purchasing, stock-order recommendations, pricing, marketplace integration, humidity, drying, statistics, and consumption analytics
- Diameter tracking, multiple structured colors per filament spool, a required or generated visible inventory number, and a separate color or material catalog
- Nested storage-location hierarchies or storage-area entities
- A bulk inventory-audit mode, general undo, or a global activity feed
- Permanent deletion of filament spools, storage slots, material units, material slots, or immutable operational history
- Implicit swaps between occupied slots
- Analytics, advertising, telemetry, remote crash reporting, and remote logging in the first release
- Reusing the throwaway NFC or Scan home prototype code as production architecture

## Further Notes

- The product vocabulary and state names in this specification are normative. In particular, a storage-slot tag is an optional alias for a storage slot, not the location's identity or source of truth.
- The current NFC evidence is deliberately provisional. The Pixel 9 has discovered a formatted, writable, 492-byte Type 2 tag with an NTAG215-compatible response, but permission to overwrite the observed non-FilaManager payload, representative placement tests, recovery cases, and iPhone interoperability are still outstanding.
- Choose the long-lived HTTPS domain, final Android application ID, and signing setup before provisioning production tags. The physical tag payload should not need to change when iOS support is added later.
- Release rules and package versions reflect research completed on 20–21 September 2026. Recheck package compatibility, Android and Google Play requirements, account-specific Console tasks, disclosures, and policy deadlines against primary sources immediately before release.
- Google Play price remains a release-time product choice. If the app is first published as a free download, Google Play does not permit converting that same package into a paid download later.
- No current ADR conflicts were found. The persistence technology and Flutter state-management approach remain implementation choices as long as they satisfy the specified boundaries and externally observable contracts.
